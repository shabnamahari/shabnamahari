# Brand decisions

Durable decisions from the content-planning sessions of 1–2 August 2026, when the
site stopped being an Adcker template and became Shabnam Ahari's practice.

`2026-08-10-brand-guide.pdf` is still the source of truth. This file records what was
**decided on top of it** — the choices the guide left open, the readings of it that were
settled by hand, and the two places where it was deliberately overruled. Where the two
disagree, the guide wins unless a decision below says otherwise and says why.

The editable source of the guide is `~/mabrandguide/2026-07-30-brand-foundation.md`;
the PDFs are exports of it.

---

## 1 · Positioning

**A personal practice, not an institution.** "I", never "we". There is no team, no
founders, no staff. Two invented team members and their stock photos were removed from
the About page rather than rewritten.

**Diagnosis, not syllabus.** The single argument the whole site makes:

> Everyone starts on the same foundation. Nobody finishes on the same plan.

Every page is a variation on it. Anything that reads like a standard course everyone
gets is off-brand, even when the sentence is otherwise fine.

**IELTS at the centre; Business English is the second line, never the headline.** This
governs proportion, not just wording — it is why Insights has five IELTS-facing
categories and exactly one for work English, rather than six of equal weight.

**The chosen homepage direction was the stakes-led one.** Two directions were mocked up
and Option A won: the hero leads with *"Half a band is a whole life."* Option B's
recognition line — *"Your English is better than your score."* — is still usable, but
never alone: it reads as flattery unless its explainer sits immediately beside it.

**The tagline is "Your goal speaks English."** It stays in English everywhere, including
mid-Persian, and is never translated or reworded. A mark that changes shape with the
language is two marks.

**One call to action: target score and exam date.** *"If you have a target score and a
date, start here"*. Never "book a free session".

**What the brand refuses to sell.** No score promises. If ten sessions get someone to 7,
the eleventh is not for sale. The honesty is the product, so it survives contact with
marketing copy — it is not softened when it costs a conversion.

## 2 · Tone

**Second person, not first person plural.** "What you do", not "what we do". "This is
what you will learn". The reader is the subject of the sentence; the practice is not.

**Warm and committed on the homepage. Authoritative and result-driven on Business
English. Honest and unfiltered wherever the truth is uncomfortable.** All three are the
same voice at different temperatures — hard on the work, soft on the person.

**Written for a B1–B2 reader.** The English audience is itself learning English. No
idioms, no phrasal verbs, no ambiguity. This constraint outranks elegance.

**Banned words** (from the guide's Do's & Don'ts, and enforced in review):
amazing · guaranteed · journey · fully personalized · strategy (use *path*).
"Learning Strategies" became **Learning Paths** for this reason alone.

**Two lines that may never stand alone:**
- the recognition line, without its explainer;
- the USP promise, without its proof line.

Splitting either one is how the brand starts sounding like everyone else.

## 3 · Site vocabulary

These are names, not labels. They stay capitalised mid-sentence.

| Use | Not |
|---|---|
| **Learn** | Services |
| **Who I am** | Who we are, About us, The team |
| **Programs** | Gallery, Projects, Other projects |
| **Insights** | Categories |
| **Areas** | Gallery *(on Business English — they are professional fields)* |
| **Blogcasts** | Blogposts |
| **Showreel** | Video |
| **Reach out** | Contact, Email, Instagram |
| **me** | the team |

"More about my programs", not "more about our services". The footer wordmark is
**ielts.** — lowercase, set to the full page width.

## 4 · Content principles

**Every page keeps the same rhythm:** header → one sentence of description → content
grid. When a page felt like it wanted no sentence at all, the sentence stayed, because
breaking the rhythm on one page out of three costs more than the sentence gains.

**Insights: no generic content.** Every post either demonstrates the diagnosis or
teaches the official criteria. "10 tips" posts do not belong on this site. The six
categories are:

Latest IELTS Updates · AI & IELTS · IELTS Skills · Career English · Learning Paths ·
Band Score Explained

Categories are named the way people search, not creatively. A learner looking for
writing help searches "IELTS writing", not a clever phrase.

**Sentence fragments are allowed in display copy.** The Insights intro is deliberately a
fragment — normal in editorial and marketing copy. The B1–B2 rule constrains
*vocabulary and ambiguity*, not grammatical completeness in headlines.

**The About page argues credibility, not positioning.** Its supporting line is the
guide's own: *"I read your writing the way an examiner does — in four minutes, hunting
for reasons to deduct."* The bio is written in the brand's voice, not LinkedIn's — the
LinkedIn profile was used as raw material and its register discarded. Three photographs
of her, presented as an album, replaced the fake team grid.

**Ten years of general English, and IELTS as the recent focus.** The experience claim is
stated exactly this way — a decade of general English teaching, with IELTS as where that
experience is now pointed. Do not round it up into a decade of IELTS.

## 5 · Colour and type

**Do not add colours.** The palette is monochrome with one saturated accent used
sparingly. Teal `#0EA5A0` and Gold `#C9973A` exist in internal documents only and never
reach the website.

Five tokens the guide defines and the site adopted:

| Token | Value | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Muted Ink | `#6B6864` | captions, labels, form hints |
| Rule | `#D5D2CD` | dividers, borders |
| Paper | `#F7F6F4` | cards, modals, sticky headers |
| Red Ink | `#C42A1E` | red as *text* (`#FF3B30` fails AA) |
| Confirm | `#3F6B54` | form success only — never marketing |

**Typefaces were corrected to the documented system:** Kumbh Sans (display), Instrument
Serif / Newsreader (editorial), Instrument Sans (body). This replaced three self-hosted
files with no licence trail — a real exposure once the site sells a service. Vazirmatn
is the documented Persian pairing.

**One deliberate exception, approved knowingly:** the footer **ielts.** wordmark is set
in Instrument Serif italic lowercase in Confirm green `#3F6B54`. It breaks both the
monochrome rule and Confirm's "form success only" scope. It is the only such exception —
it does not license others.

**Full-width display words must be measured, not eyeballed.** Setting a word to span the
page width clipped its last letter three separate times ("Business English", "Shabnam
Ahari", "ielts."). Render it and read the pixels before calling it done.

## 6 · Photography

The guide's photography brief — greyscale with colour on hover, the lighting rules, the
"never" list — **governs personal photography only**: her, her hands, her work. This
scope note was added to the brand-foundation source above the Photography section.

Decorative and conceptual images that do not feature her — category and programme
thumbnails, topic illustrations, supporting graphics — are free in style, including
illustration, and are not bound by any of it. The greyscale/hover mechanic is
specifically excluded from the Learn, IELTS, Blogcasts, and Business English category
images.

## 7 · Still open

- **The bilingual site.** The guide requires Persian for explanation and English for the
  standard. Vazirmatn is loaded, but there is no Persian content, no RTL layout, and no
  language infrastructure on the marketing pages. This is a decision, not a task.
  (The chatbot is already bilingual — see [CHATBOT.md](CHATBOT.md).)
- **Real photographs.** The homepage images still come live from `picsum.photos`. They
  are not hers.
- **Pricing and consultation.** The guide places copy on a pricing page and a
  consultation call. Neither page exists.
